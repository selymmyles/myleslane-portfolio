## Project overview

Self-improvement and habit formation are popular among young people, but many struggle with consistency. I created a habit-tracking app that uses social interactivity to make habit-building collaborative and enjoyable. By turning habits into shared goals with friends, the process becomes something to look forward to rather than a chore.

## The problem

Young adults trying to self-improve and form new habits have trouble keeping themselves consistent and holding themselves accountable with only themselves as motivation.

<div class="stat-callout">
  <span class="stat">58%</span>
  <span class="stat-label">of young adults report feeling a lack of meaning or purpose in their lives, impacting their ability to stay consistent with their goals.<br /><em>Harvard Graduate School of Education, 2023</em></span>
</div>

## The solution

**Leveraging social connection.** Bondit helps users form healthy habits with friends by creating "bonds" — groups based on shared habits where friends take daily photos to maintain streaks. It's an easy way to stay accountable while strengthening friendships without feeling like a chore.

### Strengthen your bonds with shared habits

The Bonds page is where you and friends build lasting habits together by creating groups, setting shared goals, and tracking progress through photos to maintain streaks. You can view personal and group streak stats at the top for motivation, then explore friends and bond groups below with detailed stats and galleries of captured moments.

### Journaling for flexible progress tracking

Daily habit tracking isn't the only way to measure progress, and user interviews showed many people journal alongside or instead of habit tracking. Bondit lets you write, tag, and filter journal entries that can be shared with friends or kept private.

  <img src="/case-studies/bondit/05.png" alt="Bondit Journal entry and Journal list" />

### Stay connected with your community

Bondit's social features keep you engaged with friends through a Home feed showing bond posts and journal entries where you can like and comment. The Profile page lets you personalize your space with a picture, username, bio, and follower count.

## Research

### Technical analysis

To gain market insights, I assessed existing habit-tracking apps to find their weak points and identify where Bondit could improve — focusing on the social aspects competitors lacked.

### Secondary research

I researched the scientific evidence supporting the effectiveness of social motivation in developing and maintaining habits, which backed my hypothesis that social interactivity is a meaningful motivating factor.

### Primary research — social motivation plays a key role

I conducted one-on-one interviews with five participants across different areas of habit formation to understand motivations, challenges, and strategies people use to stay consistent. Three themes emerged:

- **Social accountability enhances consistency.** Participants found that involving friends or social groups in their habits provided obligation and motivation — whether rock climbing with friends or joining a small group for spiritual practices.
- **Journaling is an important feature.** Most participants used casual journaling as their primary tracking method rather than structured habit tracking. This surprised me — I had initially planned the app to focus on habit tracking only.
- **Intuitive and fun.** Many apps participants had tried previously were unintuitive and failed to engage them long-term. They wanted something more aligned with the apps they use daily — BeReal, Snapchat, Instagram, TikTok.

### User needs

- **Journaling** — users want to journal as their preferred way of keeping track of things.
- **Social integration** — users need social integration throughout the app to make habit-forming more enjoyable.
- **Familiar** — users need a UI that is easy to pick up, similar to other apps they use daily.

## User flows & information architecture

After research, I planned out the different flows of the app and the labeling of each page. I held a remote-monitored tree test to validate the information architecture, then moved into wireframing.

## Wireframe & prototype

**I tested what was essential and cut the unnecessary.** After iterating on my wireframe, I conducted a remote usability study that confirmed concerns I had about the design. Participants indicated some pages were hard to navigate and felt overwhelming due to too many elements on-screen. I used this feedback to revise for the high-fidelity prototype — reducing clutter and improving navigation.

## Design system

Through my research, I identified key themes that shaped Bondit's design. The color palette — warm neutrals balanced against energetic orange and calming teal — was chosen to create an inviting experience. Each design decision reflects the goal of making habit tracking feel both personal and socially engaging.

## Hi-fi & final

**Simplify, refine, focus.** After reviewing usability test feedback, I simplified the app to focus on its core purpose — helping users connect with friends to form habits and journal — by removing complex data-heavy pages that weren't valued by the target audience. I refined the UI to balance familiarity with uniqueness.

The final usability test showed **100% task completion** and **60% improved usability ratings**, with participants rating intuitiveness consistently 4–5 out of 5.

## Takeaways

### Test. Test. Test.

Testing is essential — it provides insights throughout the design process, uncovers users' true needs, identifies incorrect assumptions, and ensures a more user-centered final product that ultimately saves time.

### Always iron it out in the wireframe

A well-refined low-fi prototype streamlines the transition to high-fidelity, reducing time spent on major revisions later. That lets me focus on fine-tuning details and building a cohesive design system, which frees up more time for thorough testing.
